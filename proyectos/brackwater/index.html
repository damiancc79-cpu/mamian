I

Preface

Everyone I have told about this has asked the same first question, which is whether I was frightened. The honest answer is that I was frightened for about nine days, and after that I was mostly inconvenienced.

A ghost horse is not what the films prepare you for. There is no screaming. There is no blood on any wall of mine. There is, instead, a very large animal that will not use a doorway you have shut, will not tell you its name, and will stand precisely where you were about to walk, with the specific patience of somebody waiting for you to notice you have parked badly.

I have kept this ledger to establish, for my own peace of mind, that I am not inventing any of it. The instrument readings are genuine. The flour is genuine. The invoice, regrettably, is also genuine.

II

The Property

Two bedrooms, one stubborn boiler, and a ground floor that the subject regards as hers. I have surveyed it and marked the places where things reliably happen. There are more of them than I would like.

No. 004 19 Oct 23.50

First sighting, kitchen doorway

Went down for a glass of water and there was a horse in the doorway. Grey, or the memory of grey. Sixteen hands at least, in a doorway built for a man of five foot nine, which raises a question of geometry I have chosen not to pursue.

It did not charge. It did not moan. It looked at me for eleven seconds — I counted, because counting was all I had — and then it looked pointedly at the sink, where I had left three days of washing-up. Then it left through the wall it had refused to arrive through.

No. 009 28 Oct All night

On doors, and the closing thereof

Here is the part nobody believes. She will not go through a wall if a door exists. She will use the door. And if the door is shut she will stand outside it and object, at length, in a manner I can only describe as a complaint being filed.

Closed the sitting-room door at ten past nine to keep the heat in. At half eleven I opened it again and apologised out loud, to a room. Every internal door in this house has been open since. My heating bill has been entered into this ledger as a paranormal expense, because that is precisely what it is.

No. 013 2 Nov 02.40

The stairs (unresolved)

She cannot climb the stairs. I have watched her try. She gets three risers up, thinks better of it in the way of a large animal on a narrow staircase, and reverses down into the hall looking embarrassed, which is worse than frightening.

She is upstairs every night. Both of these things are true. I have checked. I have checked a great deal.

No. 017 9 Nov 16.30

Hostilities: Gerald

Gerald is the robot vacuum. Gerald has one instruction in life, which is to return to his dock at half four. The dock sits in the hall. The hall is hers.

For six days Gerald advanced and was blocked. He would turn ninety degrees and try again; she would move eight inches and block him again. He beeped. She did not blink. On the seventh day I moved the dock into the cupboard under the stairs and told them both that this was a compromise and that neither of them had won.

Gerald docks in the cupboard now. She stands in the hall looking directly at the cupboard. I maintain that this is a ceasefire. I am aware of how it looks.

No. 021 15 Nov 21.02

The microwave incident

One beep. One. She went through the kitchen ceiling — not up the stairs, not through the door she is otherwise so principled about, but straight up through the ceiling, which is a thing I now know she can do and has simply been declining to do out of what I can only assume is manners.

She did not come downstairs for two days. There is a hairline crack above the hob that the surveyor will never be told about.

I now stop the microwave at one second remaining. Every time. For the rest of my life, presumably.

No. 024 21 Nov 11.15

The farrier attends

Rang three farriers. Two hung up. The third, Mr D. Coalhouse, said "ah" in a way that suggested this was not his first, and asked whether she was going short on the near fore. She was. I had not mentioned it.

He worked for fifty minutes on a horse he could not see, using a method he described as "vibes and forty years." At one point he said "stand over" and something in my hall stood over.

The hoofbeats have been markedly crisper since. I have paid the invoice. I have framed the invoice.

No. 029 2 Dec 19.40

Naming

Tried, over the course of an evening: Bartholomew. Shadow. Duchess. Nightmare, which I regretted instantly and apologised for. Mr Hooves. Susan. Nothing.

Then I needed to get past her to the fridge, and I said "excuse me," and she turned round.

Tried it again the next morning. Same result. Tried it on Thursday from two rooms away, and heard eight beats coming down the hall.

Her name is Excuse Me. I did not choose this. I have been overruled.

No. 038 19 Dec 01.55

Storm

Wind at sixty-odd miles an hour off the marsh, and the gate at the bottom of the garden going all night. She hates wind. I did not know that about her until tonight, and I have thought about very little else since.

She came up — by whatever means she comes up — and stood at the foot of my bed in the dark, breathing in a way that a thing which does not breathe should not be able to manage, and shaking.

Clause four of the Terms says that the subject will not enter the bedroom. Clause four was my idea. I got up, I found the ledger, and I struck it out myself before she could.

No. 044 31 Dec 22.00

Fireworks

Sat on the floor of the hall with the lights off from ten until gone midnight, talking absolute nonsense to an empty corridor, as one does for any animal on the thirty-first of December.

Mrs Ballantyne next door says she saw me through the glass and that I want to be careful. Mrs Ballantyne has maintained since October that all of this is the pipes. Mrs Ballantyne has never once been in my upstairs hall.

V

Terms of Cohabitation

Drafted 5 November and taped to the fridge, where it remains. It has been amended twice. I made one of the amendments. I am reasonably confident about which.

Terms of Cohabitation

14 Brackwater Lane · posted on the fridge · binding, apparently

  1. The subject may use any room on the ground floor, at any hour, including the ones I am in.
  2. The subject may use the stairs by whatever means she currently employs. No explanation is required. An explanation would nevertheless be very welcome.
  3. Doors remain open. All of them. Always. I have made my peace with this and so has the boiler.
  4. The subject will not enter the bedroom.struck 19 Dec — my hand, my idea, don't ask
  5. The word "glue" is retired from this house in every sense, including adhesive. Sellotape has been reclassified as "the sticky one."
  6. Gerald docks in the cupboard. This was not my idea and I remain publicly neutral on the matter.
  7. The oats stay out. Nobody eats the oats. That is not the point of the oats.
Signed, both parties

Nothing to report. Slept through. Woke to prints in the flour going out of the kitchen, as always, never in. I have stopped finding that sinister and started finding it a bit rude that she never says hello on the way past.
